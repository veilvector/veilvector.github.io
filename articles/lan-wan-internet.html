NETWORKING GUIDE 017 5 MIN READ

LAN, WAN & the Internet

A network can connect a few devices in one room or link systems across the world. Let's follow the connections and see how those different scales fit together.

01 / THE STARTING POINT

You're sitting at home.

Your laptop is connected to Wi-Fi. Your phone is connected too. Maybe your TV and printer are online as well.

They can communicate across your local network. Then you open a website hosted on a server somewhere on the other side of the world.

Your small home network is now exchanging data with a much wider collection of networks. How does that happen?

02 / THREE SCALES

Networks can connect at different scales

The Internet

These aren't three completely separate worlds. A LAN can connect to a WAN, and networks operated by many different organisations can connect to form part of the Internet.

The useful distinction is the role each term describes: a local network, a wide-area network, or the global system of interconnected networks.

03 / LAN

Start with the network around you

Think about your home Wi-Fi. Your phone, laptop, smart TV and printer may all connect through your router or wireless access point.

Together, these devices can form a LAN — Local Area Network. A LAN covers a relatively limited area, such as a home, classroom, laboratory, office or building.

Example · a home LAN
Laptop DEVICE
Router LOCAL NETWORK
Phone DEVICE
Printer DEVICE
Smart TV DEVICE

Simplified illustration. Devices may connect by Wi-Fi or cable, and the router is not necessarily the only networking device.

Devices on the same LAN can often communicate directly over that local network, depending on their configuration and security rules.

For example, your laptop can send a print job to a local network printer without sending that print job across the global Internet.

04 / WAN

Now make the distance bigger

Imagine a company with offices in Chennai, Mumbai and Delhi. Each office may have its own LAN, with computers, printers and other devices.

The company also needs those offices to share information and access internal systems. A WAN — Wide Area Network can connect networks over a larger geographical area.

Example · connecting office networks
Chennai OFFICE LAN
Company WAN CONNECTS NETWORKS
Mumbai OFFICE LAN

Conceptual example: a real WAN may connect many sites using different links and network providers.

Notice that the WAN doesn't have to replace the office LANs. It connects them. The offices can keep their local networks while communicating over the wider network.

A WAN may be privately operated by an organisation or provided through network services. The term describes the wider network, not one specific technology.

05 / THE INTERNET

And then there's the Internet

The Internet isn't one giant computer in a secret building. It's a huge collection of interconnected networks that can exchange data using shared protocols.

Your home network connects to an Internet Service Provider (ISP). The ISP connects with other networks, and those connections can carry traffic toward a server hosting the website you want.

A simplified route to a website
Home LAN YOUR DEVICES
→

→

→
Web server DESTINATION

This is a conceptual path, not a fixed route. Actual traffic may pass through multiple networks and routers.

Routers use destination information to forward packets toward their next hop. There isn't necessarily one single path that every packet must follow, and the route can change.

STOP & THINK

Your laptop is on a home LAN, while the website is hosted thousands of kilometres away. What allows data to travel between them?

The short answer is a chain of interconnected networks and networking equipment. Your home router helps connect your LAN to your provider, while routers across other networks forward traffic onward.

Each part has a role, but no single router needs to understand the entire Internet. Routers use routing information to choose where to send packets next.

06 / THE DISTINCTION

Keep the three ideas separate

LAN

A relatively small local network, such as a home, office or lab.

WAN

A network connecting systems or smaller networks over a wider geographical area.

INTERNET

The global system of interconnected networks that communicate using shared protocols.

07 / THE SECURITY CONNECTION

Why does this matter in cybersecurity?

An attacker doesn't interact with the Internet as one giant, featureless thing. Network activity involves real devices, addresses, routes, services and connections.

Defenders need to understand that same landscape. When an unusual connection appears in a company's logs, an investigator may ask:

01

Which device or internal network did the connection come from?

02

What destination did it contact, and which service was involved?

03

Was the communication expected, authorised and consistent with normal activity?

Network boundaries, device addresses and traffic logs help analysts build a clearer picture of what happened. Understanding how networks connect gives those clues useful context.

08 / CONNECT THE DOTS

Go back to that simple moment from the beginning. You're at home, your laptop is connected to Wi-Fi, and you open a website.

What looked like one click may involve your local network, your Internet provider, other networks and a remote server.

Your small network can reach the rest of the world because networks are connected to networks.

TAKEAWAY

LAN describes a local network. WAN connects systems or networks across larger distances. The Internet is a global collection of interconnected networks.
