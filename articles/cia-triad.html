The CIA Triad

Three simple questions sit underneath many cybersecurity decisions: Who should see the information? Has it been changed? Can we still access it?

GUIDE 014 · CYBERSECURITY FUNDAMENTALS
01 / THE SCENE

A system holds something important.

Imagine you're responsible for protecting a hospital's patient database. It contains medical records, prescriptions, and personal information. Doctors need it to treat patients, and the hospital needs it to run safely.

SYSTEM UNDER PROTECTION
System
Hospital patient database
Contains
Records, prescriptions, personal data
Needed by
Authorized hospital staff

One day, something goes wrong. The details aren't clear yet. Before deciding what happened, you need a way to classify the impact.

02 / CLUE ONE

Confidentiality: who saw the data?

Confidentiality means preventing information from being disclosed to people who aren't authorized to see it.

ACCESS RECORD · EXAMPLE
Resource
Patient medical record
Activity
Access by an unauthorized account
Possible impact
Private information was exposed

The information may still be accurate, and the system may still be working. The problem is that someone without permission could see private information.

In this situation, confidentiality has been compromised. The data doesn't have to be changed or deleted for a breach of confidentiality to occur.

Controls that help protect confidentiality include:

Encryption
Protects data from unauthorized reading

Makes information unreadable without the appropriate key, assuming the encryption is correctly implemented and used.

Authentication
Checks identity

Helps verify that a user or system is who or what it claims to be.

Authorization
Sets access permissions

Determines which resources and actions an authenticated identity is allowed to use.

03 / CLUE TWO

Integrity: was the data changed?

Integrity means keeping information accurate and complete, and protecting it from unauthorized or improper modification.

Imagine a medication record originally says 10 mg. Someone changes it to 100 mg without authorization. That change could have serious consequences.

10 mg Original recorded dosage
→
100 mg Unexpected modification

The database may still be online, and staff may still be able to open it. But if information has been altered improperly, integrity has been affected.

Controls that help protect integrity include:

Hashing
Helps detect differences

Comparing a file's hash with a trusted reference can reveal that its contents have changed. A hash alone doesn't prove who made the change or whether the original was trustworthy.

Digital signatures
Help verify authenticity

A valid signature can help verify that signed data hasn't changed since signing and that it was signed using the corresponding private key.

Audit logs
Support investigation

Records of access and modification can help investigators establish what happened, when it happened, and which account was involved.

Change monitoring and appropriate permissions also help prevent unauthorized modifications or bring them to an analyst's attention.

04 / CLUE THREE

Availability: can people still use the system?

Availability means authorized users can access systems and information when they need them.

Imagine the patient database contains correct information, but a server failure makes it unreachable. Doctors can't retrieve the records they need. The data might still exist, but the service isn't available.

THE AVAILABILITY PROBLEM
Database

The information still exists.

→
Service failure

The system can't respond as expected.

→
No access

Authorized staff can't retrieve records.

Availability can be affected by hardware failures, network outages, software problems, or denial-of-service attacks.

Common measures that support availability include:

Backups
Support recovery

Provide a way to restore data after loss or damage. Backups should be tested and protected from unauthorized access.

Redundancy
Provide alternatives

Extra systems or components can help a service continue when one component fails.

Monitoring and failover
Detect and respond to failures

Monitoring can alert teams to problems, while failover mechanisms can shift work to an available component when correctly configured.

05 / CONNECT THE CLUES

One incident can affect all three.

Now imagine an attacker compromises a hospital server. The impact depends on what the attacker does and what the hospital loses.

THE CIA TRIAD · POSSIBLE IMPACTS
Confidentiality

Patient records may be exposed to unauthorized people.

Integrity

Records could be changed without authorization.

Availability

The hospital may lose access to its systems or information.

Not every incident affects all three principles. An unauthorized disclosure might affect confidentiality without changing records. A service outage might primarily affect availability.

Investigators need evidence to determine which impacts actually occurred. The CIA Triad helps classify the impact; it doesn't, by itself, explain how an incident happened or identify who caused it.

06 / THINK LIKE AN INVESTIGATOR

Three questions to ask when something goes wrong.

QUESTION 01 Did someone see information they weren't allowed to see?

That points to confidentiality.

QUESTION 02 Was information changed, corrupted, or made unreliable?

That points to integrity.

QUESTION 03 Can authorized users still access the system when they need it?

That points to availability.

One important detail

The CIA Triad isn't a rigid checklist where one principle always matters more than the others. Real systems balance confidentiality, integrity, and availability according to their purpose, risks, and the needs of the people who depend on them.

TAKEAWAY · 014

Protect the data. Protect its accuracy. Keep it available.

Confidentiality protects information from unauthorized disclosure.

Integrity protects information from unauthorized or improper modification.

Availability helps ensure authorized users can access systems and information when needed.

Together, these three principles form a foundation for thinking about cybersecurity incidents, security controls, and risk.
