BEGINNER PATH 014

The CIA Triad

Three simple questions sit underneath many cybersecurity decisions: Who should see the information? Has it been changed? Can we still access it?

THE SCENE

A system holds something important.

Imagine you're responsible for protecting a hospital's patient database. It contains medical records, prescriptions, and personal information. Doctors need it to treat patients, and the hospital needs it to run safely.

SYSTEM UNDER PROTECTION
System Hospital patient database
Contains Records, prescriptions, personal data
Needed by Authorized hospital staff

One day, something goes wrong. The details aren't clear yet. Before deciding what happened, you need a way to classify the damage.

CLUE 01

Confidentiality: who saw the data?

Confidentiality means preventing information from being disclosed to people who aren't authorized to see it.

ACCESS RECORD
Resource: Patient medical record
Access by an unauthorized account
The record may still be accurate, and the system may still be working. But someone without permission has viewed private information.
The information was exposed, even if nothing was changed.

In this situation, confidentiality has been compromised. The problem isn't necessarily that the data is wrong or unavailable. The problem is that the wrong person was able to see it.

Controls that help protect confidentiality include:

Encryption Helps protect data from unauthorized reading.
Authentication Verifies the identity of a user or system.
Authorization Determines what an authenticated user may access.
CLUE 02

Integrity: was the data changed?

Integrity means keeping information accurate and complete, and protecting it from unauthorized or improper modification.

Imagine a medication record originally says 10 mg. Someone changes it to 100 mg without authorization. That small-looking change could have serious consequences.

10 mg Original recorded dosage
→
100 mg Unexpected modification

The database may still be online, and staff may still be able to open it. But if its information has been altered improperly, integrity has been affected.

Controls that help protect integrity include:

Hashes Help detect whether data differs from a known version.
Digital signatures Help verify authenticity and detect changes.
Audit logs Record activity so investigators can trace changes.

Change monitoring and appropriate permissions also help prevent unauthorized modifications or bring them to an analyst's attention.

CLUE 03

Availability: can people still use the system?

Availability means authorized users can access systems and information when they need them.

Imagine the patient database contains correct information, but a server failure makes it unreachable. Doctors can't retrieve the records they need. The data might still exist, but the service isn't available.

Database The information still exists.
→
No access Authorized staff can't reach it.

Availability can be affected by hardware failures, network outages, software problems, or denial-of-service attacks.

Common measures that support availability include:

Backups Provide a way to recover data after loss or damage.
Redundancy Provides alternative systems or components.
Monitoring and failover Help detect failures and keep services running where possible.
CONNECT THE CLUES

One incident can affect all three.

Now imagine an attacker compromises a hospital server. The impact depends on what the attacker does and what the hospital loses.

THE CIA TRIAD
Confidentiality Patient records may be exposed.
Integrity Records could be altered.
Availability The hospital may lose access to its systems.

Not every incident affects all three principles. An unauthorized disclosure might affect confidentiality without changing the records. A service outage might primarily affect availability. Investigators need evidence to determine which impacts actually occurred.

This is why cybersecurity isn't simply about keeping attackers out. It is also about protecting information, preserving its trustworthiness, and keeping essential systems usable.

THINK LIKE AN INVESTIGATOR

Three questions to ask when something goes wrong.

QUESTION 01
Did someone see information they weren't allowed to see?

That points to confidentiality.

QUESTION 02
Was information changed, corrupted, or made unreliable?

That points to integrity.

QUESTION 03
Can authorized users still access the system when they need it?

That points to availability.

One important detail: The CIA Triad isn't a rigid checklist where one principle always matters more than the others. Real systems balance confidentiality, integrity, and availability according to their purpose and risks.
TAKEAWAY

Protect the data. Protect its accuracy. Keep it available.

Confidentiality protects information from unauthorized disclosure.

Integrity protects information from unauthorized or improper modification.

Availability helps ensure authorized users can access systems and information when needed.

Together, these three principles form a foundation for thinking about cybersecurity incidents, security controls, and risk.
